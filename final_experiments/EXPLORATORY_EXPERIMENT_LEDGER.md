## Academic Interpretation

The exploratory programme now gives a consistent answer: small statistical
associations exist, especially for the share of negative stories and for the
surprise decomposition, but conditioning, publisher weighting, learned gates,
scorer replacement, sparse event selection, cash-flow-distance sizing, sector
control, firm-level risk brakes, and trading translations do not produce robust
economic performance after costs. Raw
subgroup p-values in story type and earnings do not survive their declared
multiplicity families. Null arms are retained rather than retuned.

The strongest independent return base is the sentiment-free HAR volatility
target, not a sentiment-only book or the standard 200-session trend filter.
Applying the unchanged aggregate hysteresis state directly to HAR produces the
highest descriptive strategy Sharpe in the chain (0.835 net; 0.878 gross) and
a statistically detectable reduction in downside-squared loss. It does not
improve maximum drawdown, its paired return interval spans zero, and its mean
advantage reverses after 2020. The prior-only 2013–2019 walk-forward audit
again finds lower downside-squared loss and this time a 31.59% drawdown
reduction, but mean and total return are slightly worse and the Sharpe gain is
only 0.050. Across both periods, the evidence supports one sharply bounded
future downside-risk-timing validation on genuinely new data; it does not
establish alpha.

Gate F1 remains a research-framing decision, not an unfinished experiment. The
evidence favours a bounded question about what negative-share aggregation and
sentiment residualisation measure, with economic non-viability stated as a
central result. It does not support a publisher, event-type, earnings-timing, or
neural-threshold alpha claim.
