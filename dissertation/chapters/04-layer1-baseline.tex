\chapter{The Baseline Strategy (L1)}
\label{ch:layer1}

This chapter evaluates the Layer-1 baseline: the threshold trading rule
of \textcite{mitic2026llm} applied to the daily aggregated sentiment
signal defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:data}. Its purpose is to answer
RQ1 and to establish the reference point against which the consensus
(Chapter~\ref{ch:layer2}) and reliability (Chapter~\ref{ch:layer3})
layers are measured. The evaluation proceeds in two stages that must
not be conflated: an \emph{exploratory} panel (June 2026, hypothesis-%
generating) and a \emph{pre-registered confirmatory} test whose
analysis plan was frozen before any of its data existed
(Section~\ref{sec:l1-prereg}).

\section{The Threshold Strategy}
\label{sec:l1-strategy}

For company $i$ on news date $t$, the signal $S_{i,t}$ is the
arithmetic mean of the per-article sentiment labels
($-1$, $0$, $+1$) assigned by a scorer to that company-day's accepted
articles (aggregation and screening per
Section~\ref{sec:data-aggregation}). The rule is:
\[
  \text{position}_{i,t} =
  \begin{cases}
    +1 \text{ (long)}  & S_{i,t} > 0 \text{ and } S_{i,t} \geq L,\\
    -1 \text{ (short)} & S_{i,t} < 0 \text{ and } S_{i,t} \leq -L,\\
    \hphantom{+}0 \text{ (flat)} & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
\]
with no-trade band $L \geq 0$ and a minimum count of validly scored
articles per event (one, unless stated). Entry is at the adjusted open
of the first trading session observed \emph{after} the news date ---
never the same session, which is the leakage control of
Section~\ref{sec:data-leakage} --- and exit at the adjusted close
$h \in \{1,\dots,7\}$ sessions later. Each event risks a fixed
\$10{,}000 notional; events are aggregated as an equal-weight mean of
event returns, not a funded portfolio, because company-days overlap in
time. Transaction costs enter as basis points per side plus a per-day
short-borrow charge (Section~\ref{sec:l1-costs}).

\subsection{Tuning discipline}
\label{sec:l1-tuning}

The band $L$, horizon $h$, and minimum-article gate are tuned by
exhaustive sweep on a \emph{chronological} train split only: distinct
news dates are ordered, the first ${\sim}60\%$ form the training set,
and the single parameter point with the best training Sharpe ratio is
selected; held-out test metrics are then reported for that point.
Splitting on dates rather than rows keeps every company-day intact on
one side of the boundary.

On the June exploratory panel this discipline is chastening. Across
the full grid ($L \in \{0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3\} \times h \in \{1,\dots,7\}$,
28~points) on the six-date backfill panel, \emph{no} parameter point
achieved a positive training-split Sharpe ratio; selection merely
identifies the least-negative cell ($L=0$, $h=2$), whose held-out
Sharpe of $0.66$ over eleven test events is indistinguishable from
selection noise. A conviction-weighted variant --- position size
proportional to $|S_{i,t}|$, clipped at $\pm 1$ --- was swept over the
same protocol (42~points) and was equal to or worse than the
equal-weight rule at every horizon on held-out data. Two conclusions
follow: sentiment \emph{magnitude} carries no incremental information
over its sign in this sample, and tuned parameters from a panel this
small cannot be trusted --- which is precisely why the confirmatory
test of Section~\ref{sec:l1-prereg} fixes its parameters in advance
rather than inheriting tuned ones.

\section{Exploratory Results}
\label{sec:l1-results}

The broadened exploratory panel covers eight large-cap names (AAPL,
MSFT, NVDA, AMZN, TSLA, JPM, XOM, BA) on three adjacent news dates
(8--10 June 2026): 236 discovered articles, 48 accepted after
automated and manual screening, and 22 of 24 planned company-day
events evaluable (XOM produced no accepted text on two dates and was
not imputed). Scorers are three temperature-zero LLMs, their majority
consensus, and VADER; the consensus is the primary scorer throughout.

Table~\ref{tab:l1-results} shows the shape of the exploratory
evidence: negative-to-flat at the shortest horizons, a positive ridge
at $h = 3$--$6$ (means ${\sim}1.1$--$1.2\%$, hit rates 65--71\%,
profit factors 2.4--3.2), fading by $h=7$. Every bootstrap interval
includes zero. The formal effectiveness battery
(Section~\ref{sec:data-rigor}) is unambiguous: across the full
family of 35 scorer-horizon cells, \emph{no} mean return differs from
zero at $q < 0.05$ after Benjamini--Hochberg correction (the smallest
$q \approx 0.38$, at the consensus mid-horizons), and no cell beats
buy-and-hold at conventional significance in paired tests.

Three sensitivity findings qualify even this null. First, model choice
matters at the margin: the three LLMs were unanimous on 36 of 48
articles (75\%) and 18 of 22 daily signals, while consensus and VADER
agreed on only 12 of 22 (54.5\%); at $h=7$ the LLM scorers earned
0.74--0.85\% against VADER's 0.19\% --- the dispersion of machine
readings that motivates Layer~2. Second, leave-one-company-out means
retain the full-panel sign at only six of seven horizons, so single
names can flip cells. Third, the earlier three-company pilot's
consensus means moved by 0.8~percentage points on average when the
panel broadened --- estimates at this scale are composition-sensitive.

\section{Cost Sensitivity}
\label{sec:l1-costs}

All figures above are gross. Costs enter the engine as basis points
per side plus a daily short-borrow rate, and the honest summary is the
break-even level rather than any single net figure: the best consensus
cell ($h=5$, mean $1.244\%$) breaks even at a round-trip cost of
${\sim}124$\,bp, roughly $60$\,bp per side --- an order of magnitude
above realistic all-in costs for liquid US large-caps. Costs therefore
do not drive the conclusion in either direction: a pattern of this
size would comfortably survive realistic frictions \emph{if it were
real}, but the battery above shows it is not statistically
distinguishable from zero even before costs. Adding frictions can only
strengthen the null.

\section{A Pre-registered Confirmatory Test}
\label{sec:l1-prereg}

Because the mid-horizon ridge in Table~\ref{tab:l1-results} is exactly
the kind of pattern that multiplicity manufactures, it is treated as
hypothesis-generating only. A confirmatory analysis plan was frozen on
24 June 2026, before any of its data existed
(\texttt{docs/week4\_preregistration.md}): primary cell fixed at the
\emph{consensus scorer, horizon $D{+}5$}; primary metric the mean
event return against zero (one-sample $t$-test, reported with
Benjamini--Hochberg $q$ across the scorer-horizon family); panel the
same eight names on six non-adjacent, forward-rolling dates
(25~June--30~July 2026, ${\sim}48$ events), widening \emph{time}
rather than re-sampling the same window; and a two-date August holdout
that is analysed once, after the main result is written down. A power
analysis on the observed exploratory effect size (return/risk
${\sim}0.36$) indicates ${\sim}60$ events for 80\% power, so the main
set is acknowledged as marginally powered. The decision rule is
conjunctive: the result counts as confirmatory only if the primary
cell reaches $q < 0.05$ on the main set \emph{and} replicates with the
same sign on the untouched holdout; any other outcome is reported as
no confirmed edge. Screening rules were frozen with the plan and are
not tuned after seeing returns.

\section{Answer to RQ1}
\label{sec:l1-answer}

RQ1 asked whether a daily aggregated sentiment signal, traded through
a tuned threshold rule, can generate profit against price-only and
buy-and-hold baselines. On the evidence available at the time of
writing the answer is \textbf{no --- no reliable edge is
demonstrated}. In a 22-event exploratory panel, no scorer-horizon mean
return survives false-discovery correction, no cell beats buy-and-hold
in paired tests, tuning on training data finds no profitable
configuration to select, and weighting positions by sentiment
magnitude adds nothing over its sign. What the exploratory data do
supply is a disciplined, suggestive pattern --- a positive consensus
ridge at three-to-six-session horizons --- that is exactly strong
enough to justify a pre-registered test and exactly weak enough to
demand one; the confirmatory verdict is deferred to
Section~\ref{sec:l1-prereg}. Two qualifications carry forward. The
implemented strategy trades single names (with an index fallback for
thin-coverage days) rather than the index futures of the registered
RQ1 wording, an adaptation forced by company-linked news signals and
recorded here rather than hidden. And none of Layers~2--3 depends on
this baseline being profitable: the machinery this chapter validates
--- leakage-controlled entries, train/test tuning discipline, a
corrected inference battery --- is the foundation the next two
chapters build on, and the dispersion of model readings it exposed is
their subject matter.
