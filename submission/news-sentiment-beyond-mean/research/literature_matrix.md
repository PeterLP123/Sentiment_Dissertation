# Literature matrix

Retained sources after the 13--14 August 2026 targeted search and the 30 August
2026 full-paper citation audit. “Core” sources can support a central claim;
“adjacent” sources delimit the contribution; “method” sources justify an
estimator or validation choice.

| Source | Tier | Design and principal finding used here | Dissertation use | Important limitation | Persistent identifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tetlock (2007) | Core | Daily Wall Street Journal pessimism is associated with downward market pressure, later reversal and unusual volume. | Establishes return-relevant media tone and the need to distinguish immediate response from persistence. | Aggregate column, lexicon era; not a firm-day multi-story comparison. | doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2007.01232.x |
| Tetlock, Saar-Tsechansky and Macskassy (2008) | Core | The fraction of negative words in firm news is associated with earnings and returns; fundamental words carry much of the signal. | Direct precedent for a negative proportion and for negative-news asymmetry. | Word share within documents is not the share of negative stories within a day. | doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01362.x |
| Chan (2003) | Core | Stocks drift after identifiable bad public news but reverse after large no-news moves. | Motivates the news-versus-price-path distinction and the reversal controls. | Monthly event design and headline presence, not modern sentiment scores. | doi:10.1016/S0304-405X(03)00146-6 |
| Tetlock (2011) | Core | Returns react less to stale stories, yet stale-news reactions subsequently reverse. | Shows why repeated stories and novelty are a central unresolved confound. | Requires story similarity metadata unavailable in the current FNSPID checkpoint. | doi:10.1093/rfs/hhq141 |
| Savor (2012) | Core | Major moves with information drift; moves without information reverse. | Supports controlling the recent price path before attributing return association to news. | Analyst reports proxy for information and horizons differ from this study. | doi:10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.06.011 |
| García (2013) | Core | Predictability from positive and negative news language is concentrated in recessions. | Supports regime dependence and cautions against universal portability. | Aggregate DJIA newspaper columns rather than firm-level Reuters flows. | doi:10.1111/jofi.12027 |
| Uhl (2014) | Core | In 3.6 million Reuters articles, negative sentiment has more forecasting power than positive sentiment in a VAR design. | Reuters precedent and negative-side motivation. | Index-level proprietary sentiment and older sample; strategy claims are not directly comparable. | doi:10.1080/15427560.2014.967852 |
| Heston and Sinha (2017) | Core | More than 900,000 Reuters stories show short daily predictability, longer weekly effects and slower response to negative news. | Closest large Reuters study for horizon and negative asymmetry. | Uses proprietary Reuters scores and does not compare firm-day aggregation rules. | doi:10.2469/faj.v73.n3.3 |
| Fang and Peress (2009) | Core | Breadth of media coverage is related to the cross-section of expected returns. | Justifies controlling news volume and separating coverage from tone shape. | Coverage, not sentiment; monthly design. | doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01493.x |
| Engelberg and Parsons (2011) | Core | Geographic differences in access to local reporting identify a causal effect of media on local trading around earnings. | Establishes that dissemination can affect investor behaviour even for the same event. | Trading response is not the next-session return estimand used here. | doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01626.x |
| Loughran and McDonald (2011) | Core | General dictionaries misclassify common financial words; a finance-specific lexicon changes empirical conclusions. | Motivates domain-adapted sentiment measurement. | Annual filings rather than news; dictionary rather than transformer. | doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01625.x |
| Loughran and McDonald (2016) | Core | Survey documents implementation tripwires and lower precision in financial textual analysis. | Frames measurement error, corpus choices and cautious interpretation. | Survey rather than a new return test. | doi:10.1111/1475-679X.12123 |
| Malo et al. (2014) | Core | Financial PhraseBank supplies expert-labelled finance sentences and documents annotation disagreement. | Identifies a principal benchmark behind finance sentiment models and motivates human validation. | Sentence-level benchmark; distribution differs from headlines and Reuters copy. | doi:10.1002/asi.23062 |
| Araci (2019) | Core | FinBERT adapts a pretrained language model to financial sentiment classification. | Documents the model family used by the pinned scorer. | Preprint and classification study; not evidence of return predictability. | arXiv:1908.10063 |
| Huang, Wang and Yang (2023) | Core | Finance-pretrained FinBERT outperforms popular dictionary and machine-learning methods on labelled financial text and market-reaction tasks. | Strong peer-reviewed support for domain adaptation and contextual scoring. | Their model and tasks are not identical to the pinned checkpoint. | doi:10.1111/1911-3846.12832 |
| Kirtac and Germano (2024) | Core | Compares dictionary, BERT-family and large language models on 965,375 US financial-news articles with return-based trading tests. | Current UCL-adjacent benchmark and a high-performing claim the dissertation tests more conservatively. | Reported strategy scale depends on its own sample, labels and accounting; not directly transferable. | doi:10.1016/j.frl.2024.105227 |
| Lopez-Lira and Tang (2026) | Core | Large language models score headlines and identify delayed return response, especially for negative and smaller-firm news; performance declines over time. | Frontier context for anticipated-price-reaction prompting and market-efficiency caveats. | Available online and assigned to the October 2026 issue; different models, data and implementation. | doi:10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104335 |
| Glasserman and Lin (2023) | Core | Comparing original and entity-anonymised headlines separates look-ahead knowledge from company-name distraction; anonymisation improves in-sample performance. | Motivates temporal caution and shows why historical LLM scores can combine opposing biases. | Working paper and GPT-specific design. | arXiv:2309.17322 |
| Dong, Fan and Peng (2024) | Core | Introduces FNSPID, combining 15.7 million news records with 29.7 million price observations. | Dataset provenance and scale. | Raw coverage claims do not describe this dissertation's cleaned 570-symbol panel. | doi:10.1145/3637528.3671629 |
| Allee and DeAngelis (2015) | Adjacent | Tone dispersion within voluntary disclosure narratives is associated with performance and user responses. | Prevents an overbroad claim that non-mean tone structure is new. | Within-document disclosure structure, not across same-day news stories. | doi:10.1111/1475-679X.12072 |
| Isakin and Pu (2023) | Adjacent | Dispersion in news sentiment is associated with corporate-bond returns and uncertainty. | Closely adjacent evidence that news-sentiment dispersion can be priced. | Corporate bonds and dispersion, not negative-story share or next-open equities. | doi:10.1016/j.irfa.2023.102761 |
| Chen et al. (2024) | Adjacent | News-sentiment dispersion around Chinese mergers is related to uncertainty, announcement returns and completion. | Further narrows novelty to broad multi-rule firm-day comparison. | Event-specific Chinese M&A setting; possible endogeneity remains. | doi:10.1016/j.ribaf.2024.102415 |
| Uhl and Novacek (2021) | Adjacent | A selected “top news” sentiment measure is more useful than processing every topic in a very large global news flow. | Shows that aggregation and selection can matter and provides an alternative to equal weighting. | Its topic-selection rule and market-level design differ from the fixed firm-day family used here. | doi:10.1080/15427560.2020.1821375 |
| MacKinlay (1997) | Method | Canonical account of event-study timing, expected-return models and inference. | Justifies timestamp-to-session mapping and abnormal-return construction. | Review predates text-based high-frequency panels. | JSTOR:2729691 |
| Fama and French (1993) | Method | A market, size and book-to-market factor model explains common variation in stock returns. | Defines the trailing FF3 control used in a post-result measurement check. | It is a return model, not evidence that news sentiment predicts returns. | doi:10.1016/0304-405X(93)90023-5 |
| Newey and West (1987) | Method | Positive semi-definite heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent covariance estimator. | Justifies HAC uncertainty for serially dependent daily coefficient and IC series. | Lag choice remains a design decision. | doi:10.2307/1913610 |
| Benjamini and Hochberg (1995) | Method | Controls the false discovery rate across a family of tests with more power than family-wise corrections in its setting. | Justifies correction across the nine aggregation rules and declared result families. | Dependence assumptions require cautious interpretation. | doi:10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x |
| Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016) | Method | Documents the multiple-testing problem in return predictors and argues for much stronger evidence than isolated t-statistics. | Motivates family registration, null preservation and restrained return claims. | Asset-pricing-factor setting is broader than this experiment. | doi:10.1093/rfs/hhv059 |
| Welch and Goyal (2008) | Method | Many proposed equity-premium predictors perform poorly and appear unstable in and out of sample. | Establishes temporal transport as a separate validation standard from training-period significance. | Aggregate equity-premium forecasts, not firm-level news coefficients. | doi:10.1093/rfs/hhm014 |
| McLean and Pontiff (2016) | Method | Returns to 97 published cross-sectional predictors attenuate outside the original sample and further after publication. | Motivates preserving adverse out-of-sample evidence and avoiding extrapolation from a selected training-period association. | Predictor portfolios and publication effects do not identify the source of this dissertation's era difference. | doi:10.1111/jofi.12365 |
| Farmer, Schmidt and Timmermann (2023) | Method | Benchmark return predictability is estimated to occur in short time-local pockets separated by long inactive periods. | Provides a finance precedent for explicitly testing coefficient stability through time. | Aggregate benchmark predictors and a time-varying model, not a frozen firm-news replication. | doi:10.1111/jofi.13229 |
| Cakici et al. (2025) | Method | Replacing a future-looking two-sided step with a one-sided method makes the reported pockets' forecasting value much weaker. | Shows why code must follow the chronology of a prediction claim. | Reassessment of aggregate benchmark predictors, not a test of this dissertation's news measure. | doi:10.1111/jofi.13484 |
| Künsch (1989) | Method | Introduces fixed-length block resampling for dependent stationary observations. | Supports keeping nearby sessions together in paired block bootstrap comparisons. | Circular wrapping at the sample boundary is an implementation choice made here, not a claim taken from the paper. | doi:10.1214/aos/1176347265 |
| Politis and Romano (1994) | Method | Introduces the stationary bootstrap for weakly dependent time series. | Supports block-resampling logic for portfolio and timing comparisons. | This study uses fixed rather than random-length blocks; the chosen length is recorded for each test. | doi:10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870 |
| Bloom (1995) | Method | Defines minimum detectable effects as an interpretable way to describe statistical power. | Supports turning LSEG null standard errors into detectable-effect magnitudes. | Developed for experimental designs; this study uses the general normal-approximation idea for estimates. | doi:10.1177/0193841X9501900504 |
| Corsi (2009) | Method | The heterogeneous autoregressive realised-volatility model captures daily, weekly and monthly volatility components parsimoniously. | Defines the sentiment-free volatility-forecasting base. | Original study uses realised volatility; this implementation must state its own proxy and calibration. | doi:10.1093/jjfinec/nbp001 |
| Moreira and Muir (2017) | Method | Scaling exposure down when forecast volatility is high improves risk-adjusted outcomes across several factors. | Provides the economic basis for volatility targeting before sentiment is added. | Does not validate a news-based overlay. | doi:10.1111/jofi.12513 |
| Novy-Marx and Velikov (2016) | Method | High-turnover anomalies are often eliminated by trading costs; buy/hold spreads can mitigate costs. | Supports turnover, no-trade bands and net rather than gross testing-period performance. | Cost estimates and anomaly universe differ from daily news trading. | doi:10.1093/rfs/hhv063 |
| Korajczyk and Sadka (2004) | Method | Momentum profitability and capacity change materially after proportional and price-impact costs. | Supports break-even-cost analysis and the statement that gross signal is not economic value. | Momentum portfolios and institutional scale differ from this study. | doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00656.x |

## Synthesis by claim

- **Why text may matter:** Tetlock (2007), Tetlock et al. (2008), Chan (2003),
  Savor (2012), Uhl (2014) and Heston--Sinha (2017).
- **Why the negative tail is plausible:** Tetlock et al. (2008), Chan (2003),
  García (2013), Uhl (2014) and Heston--Sinha (2017).
- **Why the mean may be incomplete but the novelty claim must be narrow:**
  Allee--DeAngelis (2015), Isakin--Pu (2023), Chen et al. (2024) and
  Uhl--Novacek (2021).
- **Why measurement can fail:** Loughran--McDonald (2011, 2016), Malo et al.
  (2014), Araci (2019), Huang et al. (2023), Glasserman--Lin (2023) and Tetlock
  (2011).
- **Why statistical significance is insufficient:** Newey--West (1987),
  Benjamini--Hochberg (1995), Harvey et al. (2016), Welch--Goyal (2008),
  McLean--Pontiff (2016), Farmer et al. (2023), Cakici et al. (2025), Bloom
  (1995), Novy-Marx--Velikov (2016) and Korajczyk--Sadka (2004).
- **Why the method keeps chronology and dependence explicit:** MacKinlay (1997),
  Fama--French (1993), Newey--West (1987), Künsch (1989) and Politis--Romano
  (1994).
- **Why the risk overlay needs an independent base:** Corsi (2009) and
  Moreira--Muir (2017).
