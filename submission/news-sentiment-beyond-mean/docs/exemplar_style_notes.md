# Exemplar and template style notes

The supplied documents were used as structural examples, not as sources of
claims or wording. They were reviewed on 13 August 2026.

## UCL MSc Computational Finance LaTeX example

Useful features to retain:

- `book`-class hierarchy and conventional front matter;
- a compact abstract before the contents;
- numbered figures, tables and equations;
- a formal title page with the programme declaration;
- bibliography and appendices separated from the main argument.

The example is a format guide rather than evidence about the current research
question. The final manuscript should use the current programme wording rather
than copying outdated administrative text.

## UCL quantitative/data-science report

The strongest transferable features are:

- the introduction ends with one research question and testable hypotheses;
- model complexity is justified against sample size and the decision problem;
- results map directly back to the hypotheses;
- robustness checks sit beside the claims they test;
- limitations are concrete rather than ceremonial; and
- the conclusion answers the question before proposing extensions.

This dissertation should adopt that claim-to-test mapping, but provide more
methodological detail and a fuller validation chapter because the MSc
Computational Finance rubric gives validation 20% of the mark.

## Longer empirical MSc thesis

The useful narrative features are:

- an abstract that states methods, results and conclusion explicitly;
- honest reporting of null findings;
- a discussion that distinguishes results from interpretation; and
- limitations and further work linked to specific design weaknesses.

Its subject, APA-style presentation and page spacing are not suitable as the
UCL formatting model.

## Adopted house style

- Lead each chapter and section with the question it answers.
- Prefer short paragraphs and plain explanations before notation.
- Introduce acronyms only after the full term.
- State the estimand before the estimator.
- Put the principal number, uncertainty interval and sample boundary together.
- Use tables for exact comparisons and figures for scale or shape.
- Report nulls in the same grammatical form as positive findings.
- End each results section with one bounded interpretation sentence.
- Avoid decorative quotations, generic history and claims of proof.
- Make the conclusion additive: direct answer, meaning, limitations and
  prioritised further work, rather than a chapter-by-chapter recap.

## Author voice calibration

Three earlier reports bearing the author's name were used as cautious voice
references: the WTI calibration report, the leakage-corrected WTI report and the
cryptocurrency strategy report. They are not sources of claims for this
dissertation.

The shared writing traits are:

- direct topic sentences followed quickly by the empirical or procedural detail;
- short paragraphs, usually two to four sentences, with longer sentences used
  when a method needs a complete causal explanation;
- lean modifiers and ordinary reporting verbs such as `find`, `show`, `give`
  and `remain`;
- numerical results stated before interpretation, with a clear benchmark or
  uncertainty measure nearby;
- methods written in procedural order and conclusions narrowed when a benchmark
  or diagnostic fails; and
- limited signposting. Transitions should name the actual relationship between
  results instead of announcing that a new point is important.

The dissertation should not imitate the most template-like features of the
samples. In particular, avoid repeated hypothesis verdict formulas, identical
paragraph shapes, neat three-part contrasts, slogan-like closing sentences and
generic claims that a result is `clear`, `strong` or `important`.
